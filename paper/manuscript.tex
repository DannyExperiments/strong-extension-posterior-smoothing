\begin{abstract}
For a finite hidden Markov model with separately specified rational
credal rows, deciding whether the strong-extension upper posterior
at a hidden time exceeds a threshold is NP-complete. The non-strict
upper comparison has the same complexity. Both are hard at the fixed
threshold \(1/2\), with three hidden times, one final binary
observation and strictly positive local probabilities. Corresponding
universal comparisons are coNP-complete. The reduction leaves the
alphabet size unbounded and gives the evidence probability a uniform
lower bound of \(1/4\).
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

\section{Setup and statement}

Consider a finite time-indexed hidden Markov model, with hidden
states \(X_1,\ldots,X_T\) and manifest variables \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_T\).
Specify a rational probability polytope for the initial law, one
for each transition row of \(X_{t+1}\) given a value of \(X_t\),
and one for each emission row of \(Y_t\) given a value of \(X_t\).
Each row can be chosen independently from its given set. The
polytopes are given explicitly by rational linear constraints
or lists of rational vertices. Their representations count
towards the input size, and the input is well formed.

The \emph{strong extension} is the convex hull of the precise
HMM laws given by those choices. The query is \(Q=\{X_s=q\}\)
for a single time and value. Evidence \(E\) is a partial
assignment of manifest values. We assume that its lower
probability on the extension is positive. The requested
bounds are \(\overline p=\max_P P(Q\mid E)\) and
\(\underline p=\min_P P(Q\mid E)\), over the same extension.

Mau\'a, de Campos, Benavoli and Antonucci leave general inference
in this model open in their complexity table and conclusion
\cite{maua}. This is catalog Q432 \cite{catalog}.
Their Theorem~3 establishes tractability for a final hidden
query with earlier observations. The later set-valued HMM
algorithms of \cite{setvalued} handle likelihoods, filtering,
prediction and extremal hidden sequences. Earlier strong-inference
hardness in trees \cite[Theorem~2]{maua} uses a branching graph.

\begin{theorem}\label{main}
For separately specified rational HMMs on the positive-evidence
domain above, both \(\overline p\ge t\) and \(\overline p>t\)
are NP-complete. Both \(\underline p\le t\) and
\(\underline p<t\) are NP-complete. The four complementary
comparisons are coNP-complete. Hardness holds for \(t=1/2\),
three hidden times, one final binary observation and strictly
positive local probabilities. All hard instances have lower
evidence probability at least \(1/4\).
\end{theorem}

The decision upper bounds here evaluate well-formed inputs
in the stipulated domain. They do not require testing the
positive-evidence promise. Equivalently the existential
comparisons can be extended to all well-formed inputs by
asking for a witnessing law with positive evidence probability.

\section{Exact comparisons are in NP}

Every probability of a full assignment is multilinear in the
separately chosen rows. Expanding each selected row into a
convex combination of its vertices therefore expresses each
factorizing law as a convex combination of the vertex-row laws.
Thus the strong extension is the convex hull of these finitely
many laws. For \(P=\sum_i w_iP_i\) with \(P(E)>0\),
\[
 P(Q\mid E)=
 \sum_{i:P_i(E)>0}\frac{w_iP_i(E)}{P(E)} P_i(Q\mid E).
\]
The coefficients sum to one. In particular a vertex-row law
witnesses an affirmative existential comparison, even on the
extended domain just described.

A certificate can name one rational vertex row in each
polytope and give its coordinates where needed. For a
constraint description, rational vertices have polynomial bit
length by solving a system of independent tight equations,
including affine equalities, and applying determinant bounds.
The verifier need only test feasibility for each chosen row.
For a vertex list it can simply use the specified index.

For these rows, a forward pass uses the likelihood
\(P(Y_t=y_t\mid X_t)\) at each observed time and \(1\) at
each unobserved time. It gives \(P(E)\). A second pass
also multiplies by the indicator of \(X_s=q\) at time \(s\),
giving \(P(Q,E)\). The comparison uses exact rational
cross-multiplication. The product of the denominators of
all the chosen table entries gives a common denominator
for full assignment probabilities, since an assignment uses
each row coordinate at most once. Analogous products bound
intermediate forward masses. These denominators and the
numerators have polynomial bit length. This gives all
existential upper and lower comparisons in NP; their
complements are in coNP.

\section{The three-time construction}

Take an instance of 3-SAT with \(n\ge1\) variables and
\(m\ge1\) nonempty clauses, each of length at most three.
Let \(A=m+n\), \(L=1/4\) and \(H=3/4\). The same hidden
alphabet can be used at all three times by putting the
following three classes in disjoint parts of that alphabet.

\begin{enumerate}
\item At time \(1\), start in \(+\) with probability \(\pi\)
and in \(-\) with probability \(1-\pi\). The upper query is
\(Q_+=\{X_1=+\}\).
\item At time \(2\), the plus branch sends mass uniformly to \(A\)
positive units, one for each clause and one for each variable.
The minus branch sends mass uniformly to its \(n\) negative
variable units. These transition rows are precise.
\item At time \(3\), there is one state for each signed literal.
A clause unit's transition polytope is the simplex on its
clause literals. Both variable units have their own simplex
on that variable's two literals. Each literal state can emit
success with an independently chosen Bernoulli probability
in \([L,H]\). Observe only \(E=\{Y_3=1\}\).
\end{enumerate}

All otherwise unused final emission rows give success
probability \(1/2\). The other unused rows may be arbitrary
precise rows. There is one shared emission row per literal
state. In particular the law uses the same selected literal
probability when that state is reached from either branch.
This is one local row in the HMM, with no constraint tying
distinct rows.

For a precise row selection let \(R_+\) and \(R_-\) be the
branch-conditional success probabilities. These average the
unit-level success probabilities over the \(A\) and \(n\)
units respectively. They lie in \([L,H]\), so \(P(E)\ge L\).
For the starting prior \(\pi_0=1/4\),
\[
 P(Q_+\mid E)=
 \frac{\pi_0 R_+}{\pi_0 R_++(1-\pi_0)R_-}\le \frac12.
\]
Equality requires \(R_+=H\) and \(R_-=L\). In that case every
positive unit attains \(H\), while every negative unit
attains \(L\). Both variable units for a variable can then
be supported only on their required endpoint literals. Thus
one literal is high and the other low. Every clause must
contain a high literal, so this assignment satisfies the
formula. Conversely, from a satisfying assignment choose
\(H\) for true literals and \(L\) for false literals, and
route each unit to a literal at the desired endpoint.
This attains \(1/2\).

This construction has polynomial size even if dense tables
are used. For every unit row the simplex is specified by its
point-mass vertices or by support constraints in the
probability simplex, with polynomial bit length.

\section{A gap at a fixed cutoff with positive rows}

Write the sign of the threshold comparison as
\[
 D_+(P)=P(Q_+,E)-P(Q_+^c,E)
       =2P(Q_+,E)-P(E).
\]
The posterior is above \(1/2\) exactly when \(D_+>0\).
In the initial construction \(D_+=\pi_0R_+-(1-\pi_0)R_-\).
Let \(d=H-L\) and \(\gamma=d/(4A)=1/(8A)\).

If the formula is unsatisfiable, at every vertex-row model
one positive unit loses at least \(d\) from \(H\), or one
negative unit gains at least \(d\) above \(L\). Indeed all
unit selections are deterministic there and all literal
emissions are endpoints. If neither loss happened, the
same equality argument would give a satisfying assignment.
Either loss implies \(D_+\le-\gamma\), since the respective
losses in signed score are \(d/(4A)\) and \(3d/(4n)\).
The signed score is linear in the law, so this bound holds
throughout the extension.

Increase the prior to \(\pi=\pi_0+\gamma/2\).
For any other rows, the signed score increases by
\((\gamma/2)(R_++R_-)\). A satisfying instance has a
vertex-row model attaining \(R_+=H,R_-=L\), so its new
score is \(\gamma/2\). In an unsatisfiable instance the
score is at most
\[
    -\gamma + (\gamma/2)(2H)=-\gamma/4
\]
in every model.

Now let \(\epsilon=\gamma/128\) and transform each local
polytope \(K\) by \(p\mapsto(1-\epsilon)p+\epsilon u\),
where \(u\) is uniform on its whole row alphabet. This is
an affine bijection onto the transformed row, preserves separate
specification and makes every coordinate positive. Lists of
vertices transform directly. For a constraint description,
substitute \(p=(p'-\epsilon u)/(1-\epsilon)\) in the constraints.
The encoding overhead is polynomial. The binary final
emission probabilities remain in \([L,H]\), including those
of otherwise unused states, so the lower evidence bound \(L\)
persists.

Couple each base model at the revised prior with its
corresponding smoothed model. With probability \(1-\epsilon\)
use the same draw as the base model whenever the histories
agree. The relevant draws are the initial state, two hidden
transitions and the final emission. The probability of any
difference is at most \(4\epsilon\). The signed score is the
expectation of an observable in \([-1,1]\), so its change
is at most \(8\epsilon=\gamma/16\). Thus a yes-instance
still has a positive score and all models in a no-instance
have negative scores. Convexification preserves the sign
bounds. This proves the hardness of both upper comparisons
at the same fixed threshold.

For the lower comparisons query \(Q_-=\{X_1=-\}\).
Before mixing the rows, \(Q_-\) is complementary to \(Q_+\)
on the support of \(X_1\). Its signed score for the
revised prior is the negative of the preceding score.
After mixing, extra initial states have positive
probability. Apply the same coupling bound directly to
\(P(Q_-,E)-P(Q_-^c,E)\), rather than identifying \(Q_-\)
with \(Q_+^c\) in the smoothed model. This gives a
negative score in some yes-instance model and positive
scores in all no-instance models. Taking complements gives
the universal comparisons and proves Theorem~\ref{main}.

\section{Exact values and scope}

It follows that computing either exact rational bound is
NP-hard. The values also have polynomial-length rational
encodings on the stipulated inputs. The vertex bounds and
the forward denominator bound give a computable polynomial
bound \(B\) on the bit length of an endpoint's denominator.
Different rationals with denominator at most \(2^B\) are
separated by at least \(2^{-2B}\). Binary search with the
decision oracle to a shorter interval, followed by rational
reconstruction, therefore computes the value in polynomial
time with an NP oracle.

The construction has binary manifest variables and a growing
hidden alphabet. The fully binary case is polynomial via 2U
\cite{twou}. Fixed larger alphabets are outside the claim.
Algorithms under epistemic irrelevance \cite{epi} use a
different extension. The assumptions here give separate
time-indexed rows and partial-assignment evidence. Strict
positivity allows the smallest row entry to depend on the
input size. The general technique of perturbing credal rows
for positivity already appears in \cite[Section~3.2]{maua};
the gap above is specialized to three hidden times and a
fixed posterior cutoff.
