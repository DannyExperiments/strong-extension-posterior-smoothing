# Q432 literature and original target

Search completed 27 November 2026. High confidence in the exact statement and the comparisons below. Moderate confidence in the bounded search for an earlier exact general-HMM classification. The report makes no priority claim.

## Exact target

The pinned Q432 asks about exact posterior inference under strong independence when the query may precede observations. It concerns separately specified rational local credal polytopes, one target value, manifest values given by a partial assignment, and positive lower probability of the evidence. The size of each local representation is part of the input. The strong extension is the convex hull of laws factorizing with independent choices of local rows. The question requests tight upper and lower bounds, without prescribing a decision threshold. The release answers via exact comparison with a rational threshold and also proves hardness at the fixed threshold \(1/2\) with strictly positive rows. The positivity promise concerns the observed event's probability under all models. The decision problem does not ask the verifier to certify that promise. [Pinned Q432](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/mathematics/probability-stochastic-processes-part-1.md#q432).

## Direct predecessor

Mauá, de Campos, Benavoli and Antonucci, *Probabilistic Inference in Credal Networks: New Complexity Results*, JAIR 50 (2014), 1–35, define their strong extension and the partial-assignment posterior query in §2. Their Table 1, §3.1 and conclusion leave general strong inference in imprecise HMMs unresolved. Theorem 3, §3.6 and Figure 7 prove equality of strong and epistemic posterior bounds for the final hidden state with manifest evidence at preceding times. Theorem 3 gives the cited tractability result for that class. Example 5 of §3.6 separates the two semantics even in a binary HMM. Positivity perturbations for general credal-network hardness already appear in §3.2. The proof here uses a direct four-draw coupling specialized to one observation and a constant cutoff. [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1613/jair.4355), [author manuscript, PDF pp. 15–17, 27–29, 31–35 and 37–38](https://ipg.idsia.ch/preprints/maua14jair.pdf).

The 2014 Theorem 2 and Figure 5 prove hardness for a branching credal tree, with one precise ternary root, a separate binary latent child for each partition item, and a manifest grandchild for each child. In that construction the likelihood rows depend on the binary child and are shared across the three root values. Serializing the branches by keeping \((\text{root},\text{child})\) in the hidden state would require tying emission rows across root values, which the separately specified model does not impose. The tree hardness theorem does not directly give Q432. The three-time reduction here passes both initial branches through common literal states, so a single emission row for each literal is shared by construction within an ordinary HMM.

## Adjacent algorithms and complexity results

- Fagiuoli and Zaffalon, *2U*, Artificial Intelligence 106 (1998), computes exact posterior bounds in binary polytrees. This includes smoothing when all the hidden and manifest variables are binary. The new hard instances have a hidden alphabet whose size grows with the SAT formula. They leave the fixed-cardinality complexity beyond the fully binary case unresolved. [2U](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0004-3702(98)00089-7).
- De Cooman, Hermans, Antonucci and Zaffalon, *Epistemic irrelevance in credal nets*, IJAR 51 (2010), give an exact message-passing scheme for single-node posterior queries in imprecise Markov trees under epistemic irrelevance. De Bock's 2017 treatment derives related decompositions and in Example 14 explicitly confines the recovered strong/epistemic equivalence to the final-query case from Mauá et al. These are different global extensions. [2010 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.2514), [De Bock (2017), Example 14](https://arxiv.org/html/1701.08661v2#S9).
- Mauá, Antonucci and de Campos, *Hidden Markov Models With Set-Valued Parameters*, Neurocomputing 180 (2016), 94–107. Section 5 computes likelihood bounds, §6 filters and predicts a current or next hidden state given evidence to date, and §7 computes extremal most-likely sequences. The earlier conference version announces the same tasks. Bounding likelihoods separately does not optimize a posterior ratio whose numerator and denominator must share the same local model. Finding an optimal hidden sequence is a different query from summing over all but one hidden time. [Journal](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neucom.2015.08.095), [author manuscript](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/17886708/manuscript.pdf), [conference record](https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/publications/algorithms-for-hidden-markov-models-with-imprecisely-specified-pa/).
- Mauá and Cozman's 2020 survey and forward searches on exact credal inference surfaced general-network exact search and ICLR 2026 branch-and-bound for maximax/maximin MAP. General-network exact algorithms and complexity bounds do not establish hardness for this HMM subclass. [Survey](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijar.2020.08.009), [ICLR paper](https://openreview.net/pdf/935e30d2ff6330e1810ecc18316f7d944706d4d4.pdf).

## Claim boundary and search limits

The theorem concerns finite, explicitly time-indexed HMMs without shared parameters that are constrained to be equal across rows or times. It allows separately specified H- or V-descriptions of rational probability polytopes. It does not cover arbitrary succinct evidence events, epistemic irrelevance, stationary parameter tying, or the alphabet boundary above the binary case. The hard family uses three hidden times, a query at time one and one binary manifest value at time three, with evidence uniformly at least \(1/4\). The strict-positive perturbation still permits entries approaching zero with input size.

Search paths covered author and institutional copies, cited and citing works, the pinned catalog and queries pairing exact or strong-extension smoothing with credal HMM, posterior-time, positive local parameters, and complexity. No earlier exact classification for this strong-extension smoothing model appeared in the checked sources. This comparison leaves unpublished, unindexed and differently named reductions open.
